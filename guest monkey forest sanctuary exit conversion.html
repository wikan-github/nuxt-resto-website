01

The Moment

A guest exits Ubud Monkey Forest. Their state is not "browsing for a restaurant." It is something far more specific — and everything in this playbook is designed around it.

Guest state at the sanctuary exit
DimensionActual state
PhysicalHot, sweaty, ~32°C humid, has been walking 1–2 hours
MentalDecision fatigue. Possibly hungry, definitely thirsty. Slightly frazzled — monkeys stole something, kids are tired.
DigitalPhone in hand, possibly weak wifi, occasional low-battery anxiety
Attention window3–10 seconds
Capacity to thinkCarrying bags, maybe holding a child — one thumb free
The brutal truth

Monkey Forest Street is dense with restaurants. If you do not interrupt the default "walk and see what catches my eye" behaviour within seconds, you lose them to the lottery. They will walk past twelve places and pick whichever one has a menu board at eye level.

Your job is not to inform them. Your job is to interrupt, answer, and close — in under 15 seconds.

02

The Strategy — Three Bets

Everything reduces to three decisions. Get these right and the page almost writes itself. Get them wrong and no amount of design polish will save the funnel.

Bet 1 — Interrupt before the walk-away

The QR must live at the exit gate, on the ticket, or in the hands of staff. The landing page must render in under two seconds on weak wifi. If the page takes six seconds to load, the guest has already walked 400 metres and is now reading someone else's menu board.

Bet 2 — Make the offer atomic

Not "we have many promotions." One combo. One price. One tap. Decision paralysis is the silent killer of exit-traffic conversion. The guest should never have to compare options — they should simply claim the thing.

Bet 3 — Close the loop through WhatsApp

In Indonesia, WhatsApp is the conversion channel. A form is friction. A phone call is friction. WhatsApp is one tap, familiar, asynchronous, and creates a thread your staff can respond to — including photos of the sanctuary ticket.

Key insight

The claim is not a purchase — it is a soft commitment. "I messaged them, they said come over, I am going." That is the psychological step that beats the walk-past.

04

Campaign Page Anatomy

The /after-monkey-forest page has exactly one job. Every section below either increases desire or reduces friction. Nothing else belongs on this page.

1. Sticky Offer Bar Mobile

Keep the offer permanently on screen

Copy
15% off mains + free iced tea · 2 min walk  →  [Claim]

Why: Guests scroll. The moment the CTA scrolls out of view, the conversion rate collapses. The claim button must always be one tap away.

2. Hero

Pattern-interrupt — mirror their internal monologue

H1
"Just finished the Sanctuary? Cool down 2 minutes away."
Sub
Offer + proximity, in one line
CTA
Claim on WhatsApp (primary) · See menu (secondary)
Note
Open now · 2–3 min walk from the exit

Why: The H1 proves you know where they are right now. That single line builds more trust than any photo.

3. Offer Card

Answer "what do I get?"

Contains
Badge (15% OFF) · title · what's included · validity window · "show your ticket" · price · claim CTA

Why: This is the actual product. It must be visual, scannable and completely unambiguous. No fine print games.

4. Why Sit Down Here

Pre-empt the four objections

Far?
2–3 min walk
Comfortable?
Shaded, rice-field view
Slow?
Fast service
Kids?
Family friendly

Why: Each benefit kills a specific reason to keep walking.

5. Popular Right Now

Answer "what do they serve?"

Contains
3–6 dishes with photos, dual pricing (dine-in vs takeaway), add-to-cart

Why: "I don't know what they have" is a silent dealbreaker. Nobody asks; they just keep walking.

6. Active Promotions

Prove the deals are real and live

Contains
Cards from the database, filtered to audience=monkey_forest_guest, expiry-aware

Why: Signals this is an ongoing programme, not a one-off gimmick.

7. Social Proof

The tiebreaker

Contains
Google rating · 2–3 review quotes · "N guests from the sanctuary this week"

Why: Low-stakes decisions are won by "other people did this." The redemption counter makes that proof live and specific.

8. Getting Here

Kill "where is it?"

Contains
Map · walking route from the exit specifically · live "Open now" · parking

Why: "Just steps away" without an actual route still leaves doubt in a hot, tired brain.

9. Mini-FAQ

Answer blockers at the decision moment

Q1
Do I need the ticket? — Yes, show it at the table
Q2
Is it walkable? — Yes, 2–3 minutes
Q3
Card / QRIS accepted? — Yes
Q4
Halal / veg options? — Yes

Why: These are the real hesitations. Answer them before the guest has to ask — or worse, give up.

10. Final CTA

Catch the scrollers

Copy
"Ready to sit down and relax?" → WhatsApp

Why: Some people read everything before deciding. Give them a clean, obvious exit.

11. Floating WhatsApp Button

Always-available claim

Behaviour
Persistent, contextual message carrying the offer and source

Why: If they are convinced at any scroll position, they should never have to hunt for the button.

05

The WhatsApp Mechanic

This is where the conversion actually happens. The pre-filled message must do three jobs: identify the source, state intent, and stay short enough to send without editing.

Pre-filled message template

Hi Tropical View Ubud, I just finished the Monkey Forest.
I'd like to claim: Sanctuary Cool-Down Combo.
(src: qr_exit)

What the restaurant sees

SignalMeaning
Warm leadThey are literally standing at the sanctuary exit
Clear intentThey want the combo — not a general enquiry
AttributionWhich QR placement brought them

Staff reply — this is the conversion

Template reply

"Great — come on over, we'll have a table ready. Show this chat plus your sanctuary ticket."

That reply — not the page view, not the WhatsApp click — is the moment the guest commits. Everything upstream exists to earn it.

06

The Attribution Loop

Without measurement the campaign is blind. The full chain must be instrumented end to end.

QR scan→ Page loads→ Offer viewed→ WhatsApp claim clicked→ Staff replies→ Guest walks over→ Ticket + chat shown→ Redemption logged

What the redemption log powers

  • Live social proof — "N guests from the sanctuary this week"
  • Per-placement ROI — qr_exit vs qr_driver vs qr_guesthouse
  • A/B testing — does 15% off or a free iced tea convert better?
  • Retargeting — repeat guests receive a different, higher-value offer
Without the log

You will know how many people clicked, but never which placement paid for itself — and you will optimise in the dark.

The compounding math

If WhatsApp claims convert at 15% and 60% of those show up, a QR placement doing 200 scans per week yields roughly 18 additional covers per week. Multiply across placements and the funnel compounds.

09

What This Campaign Is Not

  • Not a "specials" page buried in the site navigation
  • Not a discount coupon you email to a list
  • Not a social media post
  • Not a Google Ads destination
  • Not a one-off flyer

It is a physical → digital → human chain, triggered at the exact moment of maximum need, with one tap to commit.

The one-sentence version

Intercept the guest at the sanctuary exit, answer three questions instantly (where, what, how), reduce commitment to a single WhatsApp tap, and close the loop with staff plus a redemption log.

Appendix B — Build Order

Stack: Nuxt 4 · MikroORM · SQLite · i18n with prefix_except_default.

Phase 1 — P0 campaign

  • Fix the coordinate mismatch on the location page
  • Migrate promotion + promotion_category entities
  • Seed "Sanctuary Cool-Down Combo" with EN + ID translations
  • Seed one promotion_code per QR placement
  • Build /after-monkey-forest — hero, offer card, proof, map, sticky bar
  • Wire QR + UTM plumbing through to the campaign resolver endpoint
  • Update Google Business Profile — category, hours, menu link, offers, photos, Q&A

Phase 2 — P0 content

  • /promotions with database-driven tabs, expiry filtering, featured-first ordering
  • /menu with the "Sanctuary offer applies" banner
  • Home page dual-intent hero with secondary "Coming from Monkey Forest?" CTA

Phase 3 — P1

  • /reserve — form to database, returns a WhatsApp confirmation link
  • /visit with live "Open now" computed from opening hours
  • Redemption tracking and analytics events

Phase 4 — P2 / P3

  • /about, /contact, legal pages
  • Additional tourist languages (zh, ja, ko, fr, de, es, it, nl, ru, ar)
  • promotion_translation for per-locale promotional copy

Data model required

EntityPurpose

promotion_categoryDatabase-driven filter tabs, replacing hardcoded ones
promotion_itemBundles and combos (included, choice, free, discount target)

Appendix C — Asset Checklist

Seed rows reference image paths. The files themselves are dropped in later — the database stores a reference, not a binary. The UI must degrade gracefully until the real assets exist.

PathSpecContent

/images/placeholder.svg1200×800 SVGNeutral dark-green fallback with venue name and rice-field line art

Handling missing assets

  • Wrap every image in an AppImage component with a reactive failed flag
  • On error, swap to /images/placeholder.svg — never render a broken-image icon
  • Log a single development-only warning per missing path
  • When the real WebP lands in /public/images/, the page picks it up with zero code changes
Contract

The seed is a reference layer, not an asset host. Missing files are expected during build. The fallback plus the dev warning is the safety net.
